# Speaking — w13: Conclusion — The AI Fluency Framework

Model answers for reading and review. Personal stories and examples are illustrative, not real events.

## 1. How do you consolidate the four Ds (Delegation, Description, Discernment, Diligence) in your daily technical work?

**Answer:**  
In my daily work, the four Ds are not separate steps but one habit. Before a task, I use delegation to decide what the AI does and what I do. While I work, I use description to explain the task clearly and discernment to check each result. Before I publish or deploy anything, I use diligence: I test it, protect private data and say where AI helped.

**Example:**  
When I add a new lesson to this course, I delegate the first draft, describe the format, check the content against the source and build the page only after my review.

## 2. Why is critical evaluation a non-negotiable responsibility? How does a thoughtful back-and-forth conversation turn AI into a thinking partner?

**Answer:**  
AI can make mistakes that look very convincing, so critical evaluation is my main protection against its limitations. That is why it is non-negotiable: if I skip it, I accept a risk without even knowing it. A thoughtful back-and-forth conversation also changes how the AI works with me. When I question its answers, add context and give feedback, it stops being a simple automation machine and becomes a real thinking partner.

**Example:**  
Instead of accepting the first design for my page, I ask: "What are the weak points of this design?" Then we improve it together.

---

## 3. Explain why AI models are not silver bullets. How do human expertise and integrity keep software systems robust?

**Answer:**  
AI models are powerful, but they are not silver bullets or magic solutions to every problem. They can hallucinate, lose context and make logical mistakes, and they are only as useful and safe as we enable them to be. Human expertise helps us notice these mistakes and choose the right solution. Integrity means that engineers test honestly, report problems and take responsibility, and this is what keeps systems robust.

**Example:**  
An AI can write a backup script in a minute. But only an engineer who actually restores the backup and checks it knows that the system is really protected.

## 4. How would you handle an ethical dilemma regarding code attribution and disclosure when delivering a project to a client?

**Answer:**  
First, I would check the client's expectations and our contract about AI use. Then I would be transparent: I would tell the client which parts of the project were AI-assisted and how I reviewed them. I would also check that the AI-generated code does not copy licensed code without proper attribution. Most importantly, I would not hide the AI use to look more impressive, because trust is worth more than one project.

**Example:**  
I would add a short diligence statement to the project documentation and explain it in the final meeting with the client.

**Answer:**
