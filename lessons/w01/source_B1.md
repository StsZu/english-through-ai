This module takes about 10–15 minutes. The lesson belongs to the **AI Fluency: Framework & Foundations Course**. Joe Feller is a professor at Cork University Business School. Drew Bent teaches, programs, and serves on Anthropic’s technical staff. Rick Dacon is from Ringling College of Art and Design. Goals cover purpose, importance, the learning journey, and expected gains. Maggie Vo works on Anthropic’s education research team.

The course is not mainly about **AI** technology. It focuses on humans working with **AI systems**. **AI** can be more than a next-level spellchecker and become a trusted creative partner. We should approach this work confidently and capably.

The **AI** world changes extremely quickly. People may not know where to start or keep up. A fundamental **framework** guides them beyond the next **prompt-engineering** fad. The course calls this ability **AI Fluency**. It means using **AI systems** effectively, efficiently, ethically, and safely. It teaches practical skills, not only technical definitions or prompting tips. Those tips may become outdated next month. It changes how we approach **AI** and teaches us to think with **AI**.

Recently, **AI** changed from specialized technologies into interactive systems. Millions now use them at school, work, and home. This change creates opportunities and uncertainty. Some organizations adopt **AI** without clear strategies. Some people feel frustrated with systems they do not fully understand. A widening gap exists. It separates what is possible from what feels comfortable and intuitive. The course wants to bridge this gap together.

First, the course gives an overview of the **AI Fluency Framework**. It presents a useful and powerful model for human-**AI interaction**. Then it explains four core competencies, called the **four Ds**. They make interactions richer and rewarding.

**Delegation** asks when humans should work and when **AI** should work. **Description** asks how we communicate clearly with **AI systems**. **Discernment** asks how we evaluate what **AI** gives us. **Diligence** asks how we keep interactions responsible, transparent, and accountable.

Each section includes focused videos, exercises, and materials. Learners should spend much time applying lessons. Anthropic staff explain why today’s **AI** differs from earlier technologies. They discuss real capabilities and limitations. They show practical **four Ds** techniques.

Most **AI training** focuses only on tactical skills. Examples include specific prompts, settings, or chosen systems. These skills can be useful but quickly become outdated. This course focuses on core competencies and deeper understanding instead. The goal is readiness for today’s **AI** and future changes.

By the end, learners get a **framework** for their own **AI interactions**. They should gain confidence deciding when and how to engage **AI** effectively. They should build thoughtful and fluid human-**AI collaboration** skills. They should also evaluate outcomes and take responsibility for them.

The practical exercise needs a **language model** and takes 5–10 minutes. Learners work directly with a **language model** throughout the course. Examples use **Claude**, Anthropic’s **AI assistant**. Learners may use any preferred **language model**. The principles and skills apply across different **AI systems**.

Starting is simple and free. Learners can create a free **Claude** account at claude.ai. No paid subscription is required for course activities. Other **AI chatbots** are also allowed. New **Claude** users receive clear guidance with each exercise.

Reflection asks three questions. Learners consider challenges when using **AI** for specific outcomes. They consider exciting possibilities for **AI collaboration**. They also state what they hope to gain.

The next lesson explains why **AI Fluency** matters today. It covers a rapidly evolving technological landscape. It introduces **Automation**, **Augmentation**, and **Agency**. These are three key ways people collaborate with **AI**. It also revisits **Delegation**, **Description**, **Discernment**, and **Diligence**.
