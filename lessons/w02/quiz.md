# Quiz

## 1. You give an AI a document and ask for a summary with a fixed format. Which mode best describes this?

**Difficulty:** easy

- ✅ A. automation  
  *Correct. The task and expected outcome are clearly defined.*
- — B. augmentation  
  *Not here. You are not developing the result together.*
- — C. agency  
  *Not here. The AI is not acting independently over time.*

**Correct answer:** A — automation

**Explanation:**  
Automation is correct because you define a specific task and the AI executes it. The expected outcome is already clear: create a summary in a fixed format. Augmentation would involve working with the AI through discussion and developing the result together. Agency would give the AI more independence and behavior rules instead of one exact task. For example, asking a CLI agent to rename ten known files using one rule is also automation. The important question is not whether AI performs work. The question is how much of the goal, method, and behavior you define.

## 2. You are unsure how to design a feature, so you discuss several approaches with an AI before choosing one. Which mode fits best?

**Difficulty:** easy

- — A. Agency, because the AI should decide without you  
  *No. You are actively involved in exploring the solution.*
- ✅ B. Augmentation, because you and AI explore the problem together  
  *Correct. This is collaborative thinking rather than simple execution.*
- — C. Automation, because every AI interaction is automation  
  *No. The required solution is not yet clearly defined.*

**Correct answer:** B — Augmentation, because you and AI explore the problem together

**Explanation:**  
Augmentation is correct because you and the AI are developing the solution together. You do not already know the exact output, so the task is not straightforward. The conversation helps you compare ideas and improve your own thinking. Agency is wrong because the AI is not operating independently on your behalf. Automation is also wrong because you have not defined a clear result for the AI to execute. A good software example is architecture work. You might discuss database choices, API design, and trade-offs with an AI before deciding. The AI supports your reasoning, but you remain part of the thinking process.

## 3. An AI watches incoming messages, classifies them, and starts drafts according to rules you created. Which concept is most important here?

**Difficulty:** easy

- — A. versatile  
  *That may describe the AI, but not its working mode.*
- — B. augmentation  
  *You are not collaborating on each individual message.*
- ✅ C. agency  
  *Correct. The AI acts independently using established behavior rules.*

**Correct answer:** C — agency

**Explanation:**  
Agency is correct because the AI continues working independently after you establish its knowledge and behavior rules. You are not giving a new instruction for every message. Augmentation would require you to collaborate with the AI during each task. Versatile describes something able to perform many kinds of work, but it does not describe this relationship between you and the AI. For example, an agent could watch GitHub issues, classify them, and prepare draft responses based on project rules. That is closer to agency because the system decides what actions to take within the boundaries you established.

## 4. Why is access to a powerful AI system not enough to demonstrate AI fluency?

**Difficulty:** medium

- ✅ A. Because effective AI use also requires skills, judgment, safety, and responsible behavior  
  *Correct. Access alone does not create effective or responsible use.*
- — B. Because fluent users must memorize the most popular prompts  
  *No. The source specifically argues against this narrow approach.*
- — C. Because only technical experts can become fluent with AI  
  *No. Technical expertise alone does not define AI fluency.*

**Correct answer:** A — Because effective AI use also requires skills, judgment, safety, and responsible behavior

**Explanation:**  
The A option is correct because AI fluency includes much more than having access to AI. A person must interact effectively, efficiently, ethically, and safely. That requires practical skills, judgment, and responsible behavior. Memorizing popular prompts is too narrow because technology and tasks keep changing. Being a technical expert is also not enough, because responsible decisions and effective collaboration matter too. For example, a person may know advanced prompting but still paste confidential company information into an unsafe service. Strong AI fluency includes deciding whether the tool, information, and method are appropriate before starting the task.

## 5. A task has no obvious solution, and you need room to test ideas. Which approach does the source suggest?

**Difficulty:** medium

- — A. Use automation and avoid discussion  
  *No. Automation works best when the desired outcome is clear.*
- ✅ B. Use augmentation to explore and experiment with the AI  
  *Correct. Collaboration helps when the solution is not straightforward.*
- — C. Avoid AI until you already know the exact answer  
  *No. Exploration is one of augmentation's useful roles.*

**Correct answer:** B — Use augmentation to explore and experiment with the AI

**Explanation:**  
Augmentation is correct because it works especially well when solutions are not straightforward. In this mode, you use conversation to explore possibilities, test ideas, and refine your thinking. Automation is less suitable when you cannot yet define the outcome clearly. Avoiding AI completely would also miss the value of using it as a thinking partner. For example, when debugging an unfamiliar networking issue, you could describe symptoms, test several hypotheses, and return with new results. The AI does not simply solve one fixed task. Instead, the interaction helps you develop a better solution through several rounds of reasoning and experimentation.

## 6. Which action best shows responsible use of an AI agent with access to a router?

**Difficulty:** medium

- — A. Give it unlimited access because agents need freedom  
  *No. Independence should still have clear safety boundaries.*
- — B. Let it change every setting without keeping logs  
  *No. This creates unnecessary operational and security risks.*
- ✅ C. Define permissions, safe actions, logs, and conditions requiring human approval  
  *Correct. Agency should operate inside deliberate safety boundaries.*

**Correct answer:** C — Define permissions, safe actions, logs, and conditions requiring human approval

**Explanation:**  
The C option is correct because responsible agency needs boundaries. An AI can act independently, but independence does not mean unlimited authority. Permissions, logs, safe actions, and approval rules protect the system and the user. Unlimited access is risky because an incorrect decision could change important network settings. Removing logs is also poor practice because you lose information needed to understand what happened. A useful design might allow an agent to collect diagnostics automatically while requiring approval before changing firewall rules. This preserves much of the benefit of agency while keeping high-risk actions under human control.

  *No. Ethical and technical abilities both remain important.*

**Correct answer:** A — Different parts of AI fluency can strengthen each other as your experience grows

**Explanation:**  
The A option is correct because AI fluency is described as a collection of connected skills, knowledge, insights, and values. That knowledge can also help you make better privacy decisions. In this way, technical understanding and responsible behavior can develop together.

## 8. Why can a versatile AI assistant still produce poor results for an experienced user?

**Difficulty:** medium

- — A. Because versatile tools are mainly useful for beginners  
  *No. Versatility does not depend on the user's experience level.*
- ✅ B. Because many capabilities do not guarantee good instructions, judgment, or collaboration  
  *Correct. Capability and fluent use are different things.*
- — C. Because AI fluency requires using only one specialized tool  
  *No. The framework does not require a single specialized tool.*

**Correct answer:** B — Because many capabilities do not guarantee good instructions, judgment, or collaboration

**Explanation:**  
The B option is correct because a versatile system can perform many tasks, but the user still needs good judgment and interaction skills. A powerful tool cannot automatically choose the right goal, provide missing context, or guarantee responsible use. The A option is wrong because versatile systems can help both beginners and experienced users. The C option is wrong because AI fluency is about how you engage with AI, not about limiting yourself to one tool. For example, an advanced CLI agent may modify many project files, but unclear requirements can still lead it toward the wrong implementation.

## 9. A developer begins with automation, then discusses architecture with AI, then lets an agent maintain routine project tasks. What does this example show?

**Difficulty:** hard

- — A. A project should switch permanently from weaker modes to stronger modes  
  *No. The source does not rank the modes as a fixed ladder.*
- — B. Automation becomes unnecessary once agency is available  
  *No. Different modes can remain useful for different tasks.*
- ✅ C. One project can combine all three modes for different purposes  
  *Correct. The modes can coexist inside one project.*

**Correct answer:** C — One project can combine all three modes for different purposes

**Explanation:**  
The C option is correct because the source explicitly treats Automation, Augmentation, and Agency as approaches for different situations. They are not a simple ladder where one replaces another. The A option is wrong because no mode is inherently better in every situation. The B option is wrong because automation remains useful whenever a task has a clear outcome. For example, one software project might automate formatting, use augmentation for architecture decisions, and give an agent responsibility for monitoring routine failures. Good AI fluency includes choosing the right relationship with AI for each part of the work.

## 10. Why does moving from AI as a simple tool toward AI as a collaborator require new human skills?

**Difficulty:** hard

- ✅ A. Because humans must learn how to direct, collaborate with, evaluate, and constrain AI  
  *Correct. More capable AI changes the human role as well.*
- — B. Because humans should stop participating once AI can work independently  
  *No. Human judgment remains important even with agency.*
- — C. Because augmentation and agency remove the need for responsible decisions  
  *No. Greater capability increases the importance of responsible choices.*

**Correct answer:** A — Because humans must learn how to direct, collaborate with, evaluate, and constrain AI

**Explanation:**  
The A option is correct because AI can now play several roles: tool, medium, partner, co-creator, or independent agent. That changes what humans must do. We need skills for giving direction, collaborating, evaluating results, and setting safe boundaries. The B option is wrong because agency does not remove human responsibility. The C option is also wrong because greater AI capability makes responsible decisions more important, not less important. For example, directing an autonomous coding agent requires deciding what it may edit, how results will be tested, and which changes need approval. The human role changes, but it does not disappear.
